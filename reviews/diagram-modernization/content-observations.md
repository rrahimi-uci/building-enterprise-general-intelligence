# Content observations (raised, not changed)

Technical doubts noticed while restyling. The restyled figures keep the original semantics exactly; none of these was acted on. Each needs a decision by the author. Pilot phase only (`pattern-reflection`, `governance-model`, `effect-lifecycle`); the remaining 41 figures are not yet read at this depth.

## Open questions about the originals

1. **`governance-model` (Figure 26-1): the review-board edge leaves only "Supply chain agents".** The source draws `d2 -> board` ("high-risk only") from the middle domain team alone, while the board is described as "proportional to risk tier, not to team". As drawn, a reader can conclude that only supply-chain agents escalate. If any domain team can send high-risk work to the board, the edge should leave the Domain-teams group. Kept as in the source.
2. **`governance-model`: the one-to-one pairing of control service to domain team.** "Identity and policy" grants to "Mortgage evidence agents", "Evidence and audit" supplies evidence to "Supply chain agents", and "Risk tiers and exceptions" sets thresholds for "Service agents", three parallel edges. The prose message ("the evidence, policy and identity they depend on are shared") implies every control service serves every team. The drawn pairing reads as a mapping. Kept as in the source.
3. **`effect-lifecycle` (Figure 22-1): "Partial / intervention path" has no outgoing edge.** The node says "intervention path" but is a dead end, while "InterventionRequired" is reached from "Reconcile" (cannot prove) and from "Compensating" (reversal fails). The changelog says InterventionRequired is non-terminal and Partial presumably leads to it. A "Partial -> InterventionRequired" edge may be missing. Kept as in the source.
4. **`effect-lifecycle`: "Declined / proven absent" is also the target of "precondition fails" and "denied / expired".** In those two paths the effect was never dispatched, so "proven absent" is true by construction, but the subtitle is the same one used for the reconcile outcome where absence was established by querying downstream. Possibly intended; the shared state hides the difference between "never sent" and "sent and proven not applied". Kept as in the source.
5. **`effect-lifecycle`: the dashed and solid edge styles overlay two meanings.** Dashed is used for "R3/R4", "approved: dispatch once", "denied / expired", "no blind retry" and "fresh approval", which the design system defines as control or policy edges, but "approved" and "denied" are outcomes of the approval gate. Consistent with the legend; noted only because "approved: dispatch once" is dashed while "R0-R2: dispatch once" (the same action without approval) is solid.
6. **`pattern-reflection` (Figure 13-1): the dashed "fails, judge skipped" edge was teal in the source with a slate label.** It is the only edge whose colour disagreed with its label. Restyled as a neutral slate dashed edge (labelled "Fails / Judge skipped"), which is also what the author's reference image does. If "fails" is meant to be the failure colour (red), it should read red.

## Judgement calls made while restyling

1. **Reference image elements not carried over.** Circled step numbers 1 to 5 (they imply an order the source does not state; the figure's flow is a loop with exits), the bullet lines ("Provides score and rationale", "Never self-accepts", "Limits number of attempts", "Controls total spend"), the "While under cap" description ("Iterate until accepted or revision cap reached") and "limit" after "N attempts · spend". "Never self-accepts" is kept because the source says it, as the second line of the Judge to Acceptance engine pill.
2. **Chips for the four checks.** "schema · evidence · arithmetic · policy" is the source's own second line of the Executable checks card; it is now four chips (Schema, Evidence, Arithmetic, Policy), each with a small icon. Same words, capitalised, no additions. The text line is not repeated.
3. **Icons are decorative.** They never carry information that is absent from the text. Possible overreach to reconsider: `governance-model` uses a robot for "Mortgage evidence agents", a chain link for "Supply chain agents" and a service bell for "Service agents"; the first two could be read as meaning (the link may suggest a blockchain). If the author prefers, drop icons from that figure.
4. **Capitalisation and punctuation.** Source labels are lower case ("accepted", "while under cap"); restyled labels are sentence case ("Accepted", "While under cap"). "R0–R2: dispatch once" is one line; the source broke it after the colon. "Denied / expired", "timeout / lost ack" keep the slash. Tokens such as "InterventionRequired" are unchanged.
5. **Group title position.** `governance-model`'s "Domain teams · decide in context" stays at the bottom (as in the source) because three arrows enter the group from the top. The central group's title stays at the top-left, as in the source.
6. **Service vs agent corner style.** In the source the three control services are square-cornered boxes and the domain agents rounded boxes; the restyle preserves that with the `square` card variant. It is subtle at print size.
7. **Layout of `effect-lifecycle`.** The Graphviz layout put "Committed" and "Declined" on one row and the compensation branch below. The restyled layout keeps the same top-to-bottom flow but moves "Declined" under the approval gate and the compensation chain down the left; the "Found committed" and "Proven absent" arrows run upward to their targets. Direction of every arrow is unchanged.
8. **Edge colours.** Source edges took the figure's default colour (teal in `pattern-reflection`, blue in `governance-model` and `effect-lifecycle`) unless set. Restyled edges keep the source colour per edge; pills take the colour of the path.
