## 4. Control plane vs runtime plane

Detail: `knowledge-cicd-model.md` § 1.

- **Control plane** (global control tier): creation, extraction, modelling, validation, versioning, testing, approval, publication, promotion, rollback, deprecation, retirement. Writers: knowledge owners and SMEs; the extraction/curation assistant proposes staged edits and holds no write credential; the **registry publisher** is the single service identity that merges, releases, and activates. Holds no run state.
- **Runtime plane** (regional cell): resolve once at admission, then retrieve, traverse, query, evaluate, enforce — over approved knowledge only, read-only. Consumers are the book's control points (§ 6). The runtime never writes knowledge; memory (Chapter 6) is a different store with a different gate, and a promoted memory can only become a *proposed* knowledge change.
- **Interface.** Down: the KnowledgeRelease manifest and content-addressed bundle; the live pointer (an EnvironmentBinding scoped to a knowledge stream, per tenant · environment · region); the run's resolution recorded in `knowledge_snapshots`; the Hub's read-only operations `:resolve` (admission only), `:search` (Chapter 6), `:query` (bounded traversal), `:evaluate` (three-valued), `GET releases/{digest}` — every one takes a release digest, none accepts "current". Up: decision records (release digest, rule ids, verdict), refusals, runtime contradictions, superseded-release citations, acceptance defects, evaluation results, SME feedback, and the ChangeBundle drafts the learning loop produces (Chapter 25). Evidence crosses up; writes never do.
- **Ties to existing records.** The ReleaseManifest (Chapter 27) declares which knowledge *streams* an agent release accepts and the ontology major versions it is compatible with; the RunManifest (Chapter 4) records the release *digests* resolved at admission [reviewed: corrected — this is a change to the book, not a restatement of it: Chapter 7's checklist says "knowledge snapshots are pinned in the ReleaseManifest", and Chapter 27's versioned-objects row is "Knowledge snapshot". Under D3 both must become streams/releases (inventory rows 7.1 and 27.1), and the chapter must say plainly that a knowledge activation changes production behaviour outside the agent's release gate; it is therefore a change class under Chapter 26 § Make review predictable, with review depth set by change class × use-case tier, and the independent-approval gate is the floor, not the whole review. The live pointer reuses Chapter 27's EnvironmentBinding record only by extension — Appendix C defines that record as "tenant/use case/environment/region → active [Release]Manifest" — so Appendix C's EnvironmentBinding row must gain a knowledge-stream variant or a new `KnowledgeBinding` row, added to § 10]; every ContextManifest segment of kind `enterprise_data` (Chapter 6) carries the release digest alongside its source revision; every decision span (Chapter 24) cites the digest and the rule ids. ### 13.3 How the architecture shapes the presentation

The grounding matrix says which mechanisms are direct or adapted; this subsection says what that does to the *order and framing* of the chapter.

1. **The pipeline walk runs in the repository's order.** Sources are digested → a model extracts typed candidates with exact spans, dropping nothing → the candidates are typed and deduplicated into a graph → deterministic invariants run first and block on structure alone → findings are routed by kind and only the failed element is repaired, re-entering the same gate → an independent pass grounds every decision-material claim against the source bytes → typing and consistency are settled from declared evidence, with finite-domain proofs where domains are finite → rules lower completely to an evaluable form or are refused with a typed reason, and tables are proved or held → change detection aligns, classifies, propagates and replays → every step reports a fixed-denominator record. § 9 of the outline walks in that order (the § 7.2 row now says so), the model file's gate table is reordered to match (`knowledge-schema`, `change-contract`, `duplicate-overlap`, `grounding`, `consistency`, `regression`, `staleness`; then the two platform additions), and Figure 5-4's control-plane cluster keeps the order with a repair loop that re-enters the gate (§ 13.6).
2. **Six boundaries, stated the way the repository states them.** (i) *Deterministic/probabilistic*: a model may interpret evidence, propose a merge, propose a verdict or draft a change; it "must not decide whether the corpus, graph, names, or references are internally consistent", and no model output becomes a gate result without a deterministic check admitting it. The chapter states this once, at the head of the gate walk, before any gate. (ii) *Independent grounding*: the verifier re-derives evidence from the raw bytes using none of the structure extraction built; a second opinion over the same intermediate structure is not independence. (iii) *Routing to review*: human review only for a contradiction or an explicit judgment finding; deterministic defects to targeted repair; evidence gaps to case management; held is a status, not a failure. (iv) *Quality budgets*: every gate reports examined over in-release, held and refused included, and a budget is an integer floor. (v) *Executable models*: lower completely or refuse; `unknown` at a table boundary means refuse; a conflict is refused, not adjudicated; the OMG projections are fail-closed. (vi) *Change detection*: align by identity, classify by meaning, propagate by dependency, replay as the oracle, four statuses and nothing dropped.
3. **What the platform adds is fenced.** After the six boundaries, § 9 opens a sub-heading "What the platform adds beyond the reference implementation" and only there introduces the independent approval bound to a candidate digest, release integrity, the six states, activation as the publication boundary, promotion of identical bytes, rollback by pointer and deprecation. § 8's runtime paragraph and § 10's control points carry the same label in one sentence each. A reader can therefore tell, section by section, which mechanisms have a running counterpart and which are the book's design.
4. **The Harborlight thread follows the same split.** Its gate passage (extraction with a span; schema pass; the supersession-not-contradiction finding; the regression replay; the `review-held` income rule) is the part with a counterpart; its approval, activation, promotion and rollback are the part without, and the thread's prose marks the seam with one sentence.
